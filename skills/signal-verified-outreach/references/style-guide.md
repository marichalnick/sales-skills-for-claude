# Outreach style guide

## Structure
1. **Trigger** (1 sentence): what you noticed, specific and dated.
2. **Bridge** (0 to 1 sentence): why that might matter to them.
3. **Question** (1 sentence): about their situation, not about your product.
4. **CTA** (1 sentence): small and concrete.

## Do
- Write like a person, not a brochure. Short sentences.
- Use their words when quoting a post or statement.
- Hedge inferences: "I'd guess…", "is that…?", "curious whether…".
- One CTA. Make it easy: "Worth 20 minutes next week? Here's a slot: [link]".
- Subject lines: 2 to 5 words, lower-case is fine, refer to the trigger.

## Don't
- No "I hope this email finds you well", "I wanted to reach out", "quick intro".
- No feature lists, no "leading platform", no "synergy", "leverage", "revolutionize".
- No invented social proof ("a peer at a Tier-1 bank just…") unless it is real and shareable.
- No asserted facts about their internal situation ("you struggle with…").
- No internal language in copy (scores, tiers, "ICP fit").
- No more than one question.

## QA checklist before output
- [ ] Every factual claim traces to a primary source I opened.
- [ ] The trigger is recent enough to still matter.
- [ ] The person owns the problem the trigger creates.
- [ ] Nothing repeats a previous touch.
- [ ] 50 to 120 words, one question, one CTA.
- [ ] It still makes sense if the reader never heard of us.
