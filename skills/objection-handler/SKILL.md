# Objection Handler

Most objections are not the real reason. The job is to **find out what is behind it**, not to win an argument.

**Do not overcome an objection that reveals a real lack of fit.** Sometimes the right question is not "how do I handle this?" but "is this account actually a fit?". Diagnose first; disqualify when appropriate. No pressure tactics, no false urgency.

## Workflow

### 1. Diagnose before answering
Classify the objection:

| Type | Sounds like | Often really means |
|---|---|---|
| Brush-off | "Send me some info" | Not convinced it's relevant yet |
| Status quo | "We already have something" | Doesn't see a gap |
| Priority | "Not a priority right now" | No urgency, no owner, or no budget |
| Price | "Too expensive" | Value unclear, or wrong person |
| Build | "We'll build it ourselves" | Underestimates scope, or genuinely can |
| Timing | "Call me next quarter" | Real timing, or polite no |
| Authority | "I'm not the right person" | A door to the right person |

If the context is too thin to tell, say which two types are most likely and give a clarifying question that separates them.

### 2. Respond: acknowledge → clarify → reframe → advance
- **Acknowledge:** show you heard it. No "but".
- **Clarify:** one question to find the real reason.
- **Reframe:** a different way to see it, only after you understand.
- **Advance:** a small, concrete next step, or an honest exit.

Keep spoken responses under 30 seconds. Written responses under 60 words.

### 3. Know when to stop
Recommend walking away (or nurturing) when there is no real need, no access to the decision, or no timing. Pushing a bad fit wastes both sides' time and burns the relationship.

## Output

```
Objection: "<as given>"
Most likely meaning: <type + one line>
Clarifying question: "<…>"
Response (spoken): "<…>"
Response (written): "<…>"
If they repeat it: <walk away / nurture / try a different stakeholder>
```

## Examples of good clarifying questions
- "Of course. To make it relevant: is it mainly X, Y or Z that matters to you?"
- "Makes sense. Is that because it's handled well today, or because nobody owns it yet?"
- "Too expensive compared to what: the budget you have, or the value you expect?"
- "Which parts would you build: A, B or C? That changes a lot."
