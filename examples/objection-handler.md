# Example: objection-handler

*Illustrative.*

**Objection:** "We already have a security team and tools for that."

**Most likely meaning:** status quo. They don't see a gap yet, or the gap has no owner.

**Clarifying question:** "Makes sense. Is that area covered well today, or is it more that nobody has had to look at it yet?"

**Response (spoken):**
"Of course, I'd expect that. I'm not asking whether you have security, I'm curious which layer your current tools cover. For example: if an assistant took an action on internal data, could you reconstruct what happened afterwards?"

**Response (written):**
"Fair point, most teams we talk to do. Quick question to see if it's even relevant: if one of your AI assistants acted on internal data today, could you trace afterwards what it did and why?"

**If they repeat it:** respect it. Ask who owns AI use internally and whether a short conversation with that person makes sense; otherwise move to nurture.
